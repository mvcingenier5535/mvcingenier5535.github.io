☁️ nuvem-palavras-z - Create Word Clouds Inside Zotero

Turn any article in your Zotero library into a clear, colorful word cloud and a ranked list of the words it uses most. No programming, no spreadsheets, no extra tools.

⬇️ Download nuvem-palavras-z

Free and open source · Works with your existing Zotero library

🎯 What Is nuvem-palavras-z?

nuvem-palavras-z is an add-on for Zotero, the popular free reference manager. Zotero keeps track of all the articles, books, and PDFs you collect. This add-on goes one step further: it reads the text of a single article and shows you which words appear most often, both as a picture (a word cloud) and as a numbered list (a term count).

The idea is simple. The bigger a word appears in the cloud, the more often it appears in the article. A quick glance tells you what a paper is really about — before you spend twenty minutes reading it. The term count table gives you the exact numbers behind the picture, so you can quote them, compare two papers, or check your own writing.

Everything happens inside Zotero, on your own computer. Your documents are not uploaded anywhere, and you do not need an internet connection to analyze an article you already have.

✨ What You Can Do With It

  • See a word cloud for one article. Pick any item in your library and generate a cloud from its text in a single action.
  • Read the exact term counts. A sortable list shows every significant word and how many times it occurs.
  • Spot the real subject of a paper. Repetition reveals the core topic, the methods used, and the key names far faster than skimming.
  • Compare articles side by side. Run the same view on two papers and see which vocabulary they share and where they differ.
  • Check your own drafts. Add your manuscript to Zotero and see which words you repeat too often.
  • Keep your library tidy. Word clouds make it easy to confirm that a saved file really is the article you think it is.

💻 Before You Begin

You only need a few ordinary things. If you can install an app and click a menu, you can do this.

  • A Windows computer (Windows 10 or Windows 11).
  • Zotero already installed and open at least once, so your library exists.
  • At least one article saved in your library with its PDF attached.
  • A few minutes of free time. The whole setup takes less than five minutes.

📥 Setting It Up in Zotero

Follow these steps in order. Each one is just a click or two.

  1. Open Zotero. Use the normal desktop application, not the web version.
  2. Open the Tools menu at the top of the Zotero window.
  3. Choose Add-ons. A small window will appear with a list of installed add-ons.
  4. Click the gear icon in the corner of that window.
  5. Select Install Add-on From File…
  6. Find the file you downloaded in your Downloads folder, select it, and confirm.
  7. Restart Zotero if the program asks you to. Closing and reopening it is enough.
  8. Check that it worked. Open the Add-ons window again and confirm that nuvem-palavras-z now appears in the list.

📖 Creating Your First Word Cloud

Once the add-on is installed, it lives quietly in the background until you call on it.

  1. Click on an article in your Zotero library. Make sure the PDF is attached — the add-on reads the file, not just the title.
  2. Open the options for that article. Right-click it, or use the add-on menu, depending on how you prefer to work.
  3. Choose the word cloud option. The first run may take a few seconds while the text is read.
  4. Look at the result. A window opens showing the cloud on one side and the word counts on the other.
  5. Adjust and explore. Change how many words are shown, filter out common filler words, and scroll the list to find specific terms.

If you want a second opinion, run the same steps on a different article and compare the two clouds. Articles from the same research field usually share a large chunk of vocabulary, which makes the differences stand out clearly.

🔍 Reading the Results

A word cloud is not a summary and it is not a judgement about quality. It is a picture of repetition. Read it that way and it becomes genuinely useful.

  • Bigger means more frequent. Size reflects how many times the word appears in the text.
  • Small words are not shown. Short connecting words like "the" and "and" are usually filtered out so the meaningful terms stand out.
  • The list is the precise version. When you need an exact number for a report or a thesis, use the term count table rather than estimating from the cloud.
  • Context still matters. A frequent word is an important word for that paper, but a human still has to decide what it means.

🧩 Who This Is For

This add-on is used by people who read a lot of documents and want to understand them faster.

  • Students and researchers screening dozens of papers before a literature review.
  • Librarians and information specialists who need to summarize the contents of a collection.
  • Writers and editors checking for repetitive language in their own drafts.
  • Teachers preparing a quick visual overview of a text for a class.
  • Anyone curious about what a long PDF actually talks about before committing to reading it.

🛠️ If Something Does Not Work

Most problems have a simple cause. Check these in order.

  • Nothing appears in the Add-ons list. Restart Zotero completely, then look again. If it is still missing, repeat the install steps and make sure the file was not renamed during the download.
  • The word cloud is empty. The article probably has no readable text attached. Open the item in Zotero and confirm the PDF is there and opens normally.
  • Only a handful of words appear. Very short documents produce very small clouds. Try a longer article.
  • The window looks frozen. Large PDFs take longer to process. Give it up to a minute before clicking anything else.
  • Windows blocks the download. When a browser warns about a file, choose to keep it — this is a standard message for files downloaded outside an app store.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free and open source, and there is no account to create.

No. The text is read on your own computer. Nothing leaves your machine.

Can I use it on more than one article?

Yes, as many as you like. Each article is analyzed separately, one at a time.

Will it change my library?

No. It only reads. Your references, notes, and attachments stay exactly as they are.

Does it work in languages other than English?

Yes. It counts words as they appear in the document, including Portuguese and other languages.

Can I remove it later?

Yes. Open the Add-ons window in Zotero, select nuvem-palavras-z, and remove it. Everything goes back to how it was.
